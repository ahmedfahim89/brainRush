## Fact-check sources

- 1125-01: Fortune, 14 Oct 2015, "Tesla Autopilot is here", and Car Magazine, "Look, no hands! Tesla's Autopilot mode is here" (both read from search-result summaries, not opened in full; Top Gear and Industry Week appeared too): Autopilot arrived with Tesla software 7.0 in October 2015, steering within the lane and managing speed with cameras, radar and sensors, with the driver responsible. Sentry Mode and Ludicrous Mode are real Tesla features that do not steer (general knowledge, not separately sourced).
- 1125-02: Wikipedia "Bluey (TV series)" (opened): premiered 1 October 2018 on ABC Kids, Ludo Studio, Brisbane; Blue Heeler puppy; family Bandit, Chilli, Bingo. Second source: the official show site (bluey.tv, "About Bluey") and a Screen Queensland media release on the ABC Kids premiere on 1 Oct, both from search-result summaries (not opened in full).
- 1125-03: Wikimedia Commons "Flag of Thailand" (opened: file page and SVG source, 3:2, three colours). Second source: Britannica "flag of Thailand" and the CIA World Factbook flag entry, from search-result summaries: horizontal stripes red, white, blue (double width), white, red, ratio 2:3; the Factbook notes it resembles Costa Rica's with blue and red reversed.
- 1125-04: Wikipedia and Britannica "Jason Statham" (listed in search results; the summary of Wikipedia, Olympics.com and Sky Sports coverage was read, pages not opened in full): English actor, competed for England at the 1990 Commonwealth Games as a diver; lead role Frank Martin in "The Transporter" (2002); Deckard Shaw in the "Fast & Furious" films from 2013. Second source: Olympics.com, "Jason Statham's diving career". Wrong options Tom Hardy (English) and Gerard Butler (Scottish) are British actors with no part in these roles.
- 1125-05: Wikimedia Commons file page (opened for licence data; the picture itself was viewed and shows the east front of Buckingham Palace with its main gates). The Commons file title also names it. Wikipedia "Buckingham Palace" and the royal.uk page are the standard references but were not opened; the building is unmistakable. Wrong options are real royal palaces, not other names of the same place.
- 1125-06: Wikipedia "Air fryer": Philips introduced the Airfryer at IFA Berlin in 2010, invented by Dutch engineer Fred van der Weij. Second source: New Atlas, "Philips debuts the Airfryer", 2 Sep 2010 (IFA Berlin, little or no oil, 80% less fat). The "first air fryer" claim was deliberately avoided (an older Italian hot-air fryer is claimed).
- 1125-07: Wikipedia "Amazon Kindle" (opened): first Kindle released 19 Nov 2007, developed by Amazon with its Lab126 subsidiary. Second source: Amazon press release "Introducing Amazon Kindle" (press.aboutamazon.com) and aboutamazon.com "A look back at 10 years of Amazon Kindle", both seen only as search-result listings (not opened in full), plus the Wikipedia "Amazon Kindle devices" listing.
- 1125-08: ECB, "Initial changeover (2002)" and the ECB press release of 2 Jan 2002 (search-result summaries): euro notes and coins entered circulation on 1 January 2002 in 12 euro area countries. Second source: Wikipedia "Euro" (opened, first 100000 characters): introduced 1 Jan 1999 as an electronic currency (the 2002 cash date was in the part that was cut off). The 12 countries are Belgium, Germany, Ireland, Greece, Spain, France, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Austria, Portugal and Finland, which covers the French franc and the German mark.
- 1125-09: Wikipedia "Gladiator (2000 film)": 2000, directed by Ridley Scott, Russell Crowe as Maximus Decimus Meridius, a Roman general who is betrayed and becomes a gladiator; Joaquin Phoenix as Commodus. Second source: Britannica "Gladiator" (film by Scott): 2000, Scott, Crowe as Maximus, a general.
- 1125-10: CERN, "The Higgs boson" (opened): ATLAS and CMS announced the discovery of a new particle on 4 July 2012 (the page does not mention the "God particle" nickname). Nickname and July 2012 date: Britannica "Higgs boson" (page returned 403, read from the search-result summary: popularly called the "God particle" after Leon Lederman's book) and National Geographic / Live Science articles from the same search.
