**Notes on the four new 400s**

- **"Ninja" in the Donatello question.** "Ninja" is an existing answer (Gen Z 600, the streamer Tyler Blevins). The question keeps the owner's wording, since "Ninja Turtle" does not point to that answer, and the Gen Z 300 Naruto question already says "anime ninja". "Donatello" itself is not an answer or a word in any question. The question does not name Leonardo (Leonardo da Vinci is a History 100 answer) or say "Renaissance" (an owner removal).
- **"Bell" in Tinker Bell.** "Alexander Graham Bell" is a Technology 200 answer. Only the word "Bell" is shared, and neither question names the other's answer (like "Silver" and "Seven" in point 1 below). Swap this one out if you want a stricter rule.
- **Bakkar without saying "Bakkar".** "Bakkar" is the Egyptians 200 answer ("Which Egyptian Ramadan cartoon follows a Nubian boy and his pet goat, Rashida?"), so the Hassouna question describes the show instead of naming it, and does not name Rashida. "Nubia" was not used as the answer because that Egyptians question says "Nubian boy". "Ramadan" is left out of the wording because it is an Egyptians 500 answer. If both questions come up in one game, the Egyptians question tells players the show is "Bakkar", but it does not give away Hassouna.
- **Difficulty.** Hassouna and Tinker Bell are on the easy side for 400, as the owner asked. Dee Dee (Cartoon Network, 1996-2003) is best known to players who grew up in the late 1990s and 2000s.
- **Mask colours.** The turtles all wore red masks in the original 1984 comics. The purple mask comes from the 1987 cartoon and every cartoon and film since, which is what players remember.

- **Difficulty:** every show is a title most Arab adults of that generation know. The 100s ask the headline fact (what Hamtaro is, what Popeye eats, the Arabic name of Captain Tsubasa). The 300s and 400s ask a main character or object (Shinichi Kudo, the Mystery Machine, Lumiere). The 500s ask a side character or the source novel. The 600s ask the creator, the source author or the Arabic voice and singer behind a famous show.
- **One question per show, mostly.** A show appears twice only when neither question gives the other away: Grendizer (Duke Fleed at 300, the dubbing voice and singer at 600), Detective Conan (Shinichi at 300, the creator at 600), Pokemon (Pikachu at 100, Team Rocket at 400), Scooby-Doo (van at 400, breed at 500, also named in the Hanna-Barbera question), Captain Tsubasa (Arabic name at 100, creator at 600), The Jungle Book (Baloo at 200, King Louie at 600) and Sesame Street (Iftah Ya Simsim at 100, Alam Simsim at 400).
- **Duplicate check:** against all 691 questions in the local DB (11 categories). Read-only SELECTs only, plus a script that compares each new answer with every existing answer and question text, and each existing answer with every new question text.
  - No new answer repeats an answer in the bank, and the 72 answers are unique within the set.
  - Left out because they are already answers: Bakkar and its goat Rashida (Egyptians 200), SpongeBob (Gen Z 100), Naruto (Gen Z 300), Studio Ghibli (Gen Z 200), Woody and Buzz Lightyear (Toy Story), Shrek, Aladdin, The Lion King, Finding Nemo, Monsters, Inc., Baymax, Po (Kung Fu Panda, which also rules out the Teletubbies), Fatouta (Egyptians 200), Abla Fahita (Egyptians 100) and Pokemon Go (Gen Z 300). Owner-removed topics not reused: WALL-E, Inside Out, Gru/Minions and Mr. Bean (Movies).
  - Wording chosen so that no new question names an existing answer:
    - "Pokemon" is never said (Pokemon Go is a Gen Z answer): "the anime that follows trainer Ash Ketchum".
    - "mouse" is never said (The mouse is a Technology answer): Tom and Jerry uses "rodent", Steamboat Willie says "Disney's Mickey".
    - "Ninja" is never said (Ninja is a Gen Z answer), and the turtles question leaves out "Leonardo" (Leonardo da Vinci is a History answer) and "four" (Four is a Science answer).
    - "football" is not said in the Captain Majid question (Football is a Sports answer).
    - No "101 Dalmatians" (101 is a Technology answer), no "2005" for Al Shamshoon (YouTube's year, Technology), no "1912" for Daddy-Long-Legs (Titanic, History).
    - No country or nationality words that are Flags or Geography answers: no "Kuwait" (Iftah Ya Simsim says "the Gulf"), "Lebanese" (Sami Clark, Jihad El-Atrash), "Swiss" (Heidi says "the Alps"), "Sweden" (Nils), "French" (Remi), "Japanese" (Japanese is a Gen Z answer), "British" (Phil Collins), "London" (Sally) or "Mexican".
    - Phil Collins: the song "You'll Be in My Heart" is not named ("The heart" is a Science answer).
    - "Studio Ghibli" is not named in the Miyazaki question ("Spirited Away" is used instead).
  - Within the set, no question gives away another answer. Remy (Ratatouille) was dropped because it sounds exactly like "Remi", which is named in the Hector Malot question.
- English text is plain ASCII with no semicolons, pipes or backslashes ("Herge" without the accent). Every question is under 250 characters (the longest is 148). Every row has Arabic text written as natural Arabic, using the Arabic titles people know (كابتن ماجد، غرندايزر، المحقق كونان، زينة ونحول، السنافر، بطوط).

**Points for the owner to decide**

1. **Two answers contain an existing answer as a word.** "Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs" contains "Seven" (Science 100, colours of the rainbow), and "Long John Silver" contains "Silver" (Science 600). Neither gives the other question away. The "accept Silver" variant was left out on purpose. Remove either one if you want a stricter rule.
2. **Doubled-up formats.** Two "which manga artist created ..." questions at 600 (Aoyama, Takahashi) and two Grendizer questions at 600 (Sami Clark, Jihad El-Atrash). Two dog-breed questions (beagle 400, Great Dane 500) and two Sesame Street questions (100, 400). Keep one of each pair if they feel repetitive.
3. **600s that may be too hard for some groups:** Gosho Aoyama, Yoichi Takahashi, Hector Malot and Herge are creator or author names. The shows are familiar, but the names are less known. Sami Clark, Jihad El-Atrash and Abla Fadila's programme are well known to Arab and Egyptian players over about 35, less to younger ones.
4. **Zina wa Nahool in Arabic:** "نحول" sounds like "نحل" (bees), so the Arabic version is easier than the English one. It is a 200, so that is acceptable, but swap it if you prefer.
5. **"Abla Fadila" and "Abla Fahita".** The Ghenwa w Hadouta question (600) names "Abla Fadila", and "Abla Fahita" is an Egyptians 100 answer. Only the word "Abla" is shared, so it does not give that answer away.
6. **Gulf-made shows:** Iftah Ya Simsim (Kuwait) and Spacetoon (Bahrain, then Dubai) were watched across the Arab world, Egypt included, but Gulf and Levant players will know them better than some Egyptian players.
7. **Fawazir:** no "Fawazir for kids" question is included. Fatouta (Samir Ghanem) is already an Egyptians answer. "Ammo Fouad" was dropped because Fouad El-Mohandes is named in the Egyptians 400 question (Sek Ala Banatak). Nelly and Sherihan's Fawazir were aimed at adults.
8. **Content:** no violent, political or religious content. Grendizer's link to the Palestinian story (Arab News, Al Majalla) is not mentioned. Iftah Ya Simsim's end in 1990 is not mentioned. The Prince of Egypt was left out (religious subject).
